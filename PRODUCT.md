# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Marcin is the primary author and gardener. Readers are peers, collaborators, friends, and strangers who want to browse unfinished thinking, links, objects, images, and longer notes without being pushed through a chronological feed. Agents such as Hermes are secondary users: they should be able to route raw captures into the correct markdown files without changing the human voice.

## Product Purpose

This is a public personal digital garden: a markdown-tended, Astro-built site for fragments that compound over time. Success means the site feels calm and alive, supports quick capture, publishes in-progress thinking without shame, and stays legible enough that both humans and agents can maintain it.

## Brand Personality

Considered, warm, alive. The garden should feel like a physical scrapbook or wunderkammer, not a CMS, blog, productivity app, or marketing page.

## Anti-references

Avoid reverse-chronological blog energy, hard grids of identical cards, generic SSG themes, hero sections, SaaS chrome, icon-heavy label systems, pure black on pure white, and anything that makes unfinished notes feel like failed essays.

## Design Principles

The garden is ordered by relationship and texture, not by recency alone.

Markdown is the authoring interface. The repository should be obvious to edit by hand.

The design should make fragments feel legitimate. A 40-word seedling gets a home, not a warning label.

Static output is a feature. Mosaic, list, notes, tags, feeds, and detail pages should work without client-side routing.

Agents accelerate capture but do not replace Marcin's thinking.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Reading is the core interaction, so type size, contrast, measure, focus states, semantic HTML, reduced-motion support, and factual alt text are first-order constraints. The site should target WCAG AA at minimum, with body text contrast exceeding that where possible.
