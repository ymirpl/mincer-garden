The economy of attention is older than the internet. Cathedrals, market squares, shop windows, the proscenium arch: every era invents architectures to make us look.

What feels strange about ours is how thoroughly the architecture has retreated into the interface. The room no longer tells you where to stand. The rectangle in your hand does.

This is why I keep coming back to gardens as a metaphor. A garden does not demand sequence. It lets a visitor drift, stop, return, and miss things. That is a better shape for thinking than the feed.

> A garden is just dirt, considered.

The work is to make attention feel voluntary again. Not frictionless. Not optimized. Voluntary.
